SECTION I: TACTICAL FLIGHT DOCTRINES

1. Energy vs. Turn Fighting (Boom & Zoom)

The Mitsubishi A6M Zero has an ultra-light wing loading giving it an unmatched turn radius below 200 knots. Never engage a Zero in a horizontal turning fight. American fighters (Wildcat, Warhawk, Hellcat, Corsair) possess high structural weight, armor plating, and heavy wing loadings. They dive faster, retain energy, and hit with massive kinetic energy.

The Rule: Dive from altitude, make a high-speed firing pass ("Boom"), and pull up smoothly into a climbing zoom ("Zoom"). If you miss, do not turn back—continue your high-speed climb until out of reach.

2. The Thach Weave (Tactical Wingman S-Turns)

Invented by Lt. Cdr. John S. Thach. When an agile enemy attaches to your tail, you and your wingman fly parallel paths approximately 300 yards apart. When the enemy closes, both planes turn sharply inward toward each other in continuous S-curves. As you cross flight paths, your wingman gets an unevadable direct head-on deflection shot right across your tail.

3. High-G Deflection Shooting

Do not shoot directly at an enemy banking away from you. You must "lead" the target by firing machine gun bursts into the empty air ahead of the enemy's nose. Higher Gunnery Skill significantly tightens your bullet dispersion and snapshot critical hits.

4. The 70° Screaming Dive Bombing Technique (SBD Dauntless)

Approach target fleet at 12,000 feet. Spot the carrier deck. Deploy perforated dive brakes to prevent structural breakup. Roll inverted, pull the nose down into a 70° near-vertical dive. Line up the bombsight reticle. Withstand the roaring slipstream, release ordnance at 1,500 feet, and haul back on the stick for a grueling 5-to-6G pullout.

5. Naval Torpedo Drop Envelope (Mark 13)

The early-war Mark 13 torpedo will break apart or sink if dropped improperly. You must maintain 100 to 120 feet altitude and 105 to 115 knots airspeed in level flight for 10 seconds through brutal 25mm shipboard anti-aircraft flak to achieve an armed run.

SECTION II: CARRIER OPERATIONS & LSO PADDLE SIGNALS

The Landing Signal Officer (LSO) stands on the port stern platform with high-visibility paddles:

  • Paddles Level & Horizontal: "On Glideslope / Approach OK". Maintain current airspeed and descent rate.
  • Paddles High Above Head: "Too High / Dip Nose". Reduce throttle slightly to settle the aircraft.
  • Paddles Low Near Knees: "Too Low / Add Throttle!" Add immediate engine manifold pressure to avoid crashing into the stern ramp ("Ramp Strike").
  • Paddles Slashing Across Body: "CUT ENGINE!" Cut throttle to idle immediately and drop into the arresting wires.
  • Paddles Waving Over Head Rapidly: "WAVE OFF!" Go-around immediately! Full throttle, gear and flaps up, clear the deck!

Arresting Gear: Four steel cables stretch across the carrier deck. Catching the #3 Wire is the ideal naval trap. Catching the #1 wire is dangerously close to the stern; catching the #4 wire is dangerously close to the crash barrier.
